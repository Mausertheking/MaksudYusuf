About

Defense as a way of thinking

What interests me most is where technology meets philosophy and ecology: not only how to build something, but whether to, and for whom.

I'm a student at Landau School in Baku, where I study in English. Last year I was the elected school vice president and editor of the school newspaper, two jobs that taught me the same thing from different sides: leadership is mostly listening, and good writing is mostly rewriting.

I'm a self-taught developer, and I build for the city I live in: an app that teaches people how to recycle, a windcatcher that could cool a room without the grid, a lap timer that measures the fuel and CO₂ of every kart lap, and a football app for girls and women in Azerbaijan that is now on the App Store.

I'm applying to study cybersecurity. Goalkeeping taught me to read the play before the shot, position early, and stay calm when everything happens at once. Security asks for exactly the same instincts.

    Football

    The last line of defense

    I've played football since I was six and trained in the BJK (Beşiktaş) Academy youth system from that age. I've kept goal for my school team, the Landau Lions, and tournaments abroad shaped my confidence and how I see the world.

    A goalkeeper doesn't get to make mistakes quietly. You learn accountability fast, you learn to communicate because you see the whole field, and you learn that ninety minutes of concentration matter more than ninety seconds of brilliance.

    A striker can miss ten chances and be a hero for scoring one. A goalkeeper, like a security engineer, has to be right every time.

    Music

    Scales before songs

    I hold a music school diploma in guitar. Years of scales taught me the goal-line lesson again: mastery is unglamorous daily repetition, until one day it sounds like music.

    Football is also why I built Verve, an app that teaches girls and women in Azerbaijan to play.

    Why cybersecurity

    Trust, made of math

    What pulled me into security wasn't hacking scenes from movies. It was the realization that trust itself can be engineered. When you message a friend, pay online, or update your phone, you're trusting mathematics no one can cheat. Cryptography is where philosophy becomes engineering: questions about identity, proof and truth, answered in code.

    My favorite example is the cryptographic hash, a fingerprint for information. It's the same mechanism that signs this page in the footer. Try it on the right.

    Encoding vs. cryptography

    Type anything, then change a single letter, and compare what happens on the two lines. Both are computed live in your browser.

    1 · Hex encoding — reversible

    2 · SHA-256 fingerprint — one-way

    The first line is encoding: it hides nothing, and run backward it gives your text back. The second is cryptography: it can't be run backward, and one changed letter rewrites about half of it. Knowing the difference is where security starts.
